The single most important fact about buying off-plan in Dubai is that your money does not go to the developer. It goes to a RERA-regulated escrow account, at a bank, in the project's name — and it leaves that account only against certified construction progress.

## What the law holds

Law No. 8 of 2007 requires every off-plan project to register its escrow account before selling a single unit. Buyer payments land in that account. The developer draws against it only when an independent engineer certifies the corresponding construction milestone — typically foundation, structure by stage, envelope, services, completion.

## What it means in failure

If a project stalls, the account is frozen, not lost. RERA's cancellation process returns escrowed funds to buyers by decision of the committee — a process with an imperfect but real track record across the 2009 and 2020 stress tests. The mechanism is why Dubai's off-plan market survived both.

## The two questions that matter

First: **is the escrow account registered and funded?** The account number appears on the sales agreement; its registration is verifiable with RERA directly. Second: **what is the milestone schedule?** A schedule weighted heavily toward early milestones front-loads your exposure; a completion-weighted plan does the opposite.
