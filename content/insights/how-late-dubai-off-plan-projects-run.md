Handover dates in off-plan marketing are projections, not commitments, and the market's own delivery record makes that plain. Roughly 40–50% of Dubai off-plan projects experience some delay, averaging around 8.5 months across the market in 2024–2026.

That figure is worth reading twice, because two things are true at once.

## It is improving

The 8.5-month average is down from roughly 12 months in the 2018–2020 period. Escrow discipline, DLD oversight and a maturing developer base have all moved the number in the right direction. Anyone describing Dubai off-plan as structurally unreliable is working from an older market.

## The average hides the spread

Averages flatten exactly the information a buyer needs. Delays at smaller-tier developers regularly exceed 10 to 18 months, while established developers with repeat delivery histories cluster far tighter. Two projects with the same advertised handover quarter can carry very different real risk, and nothing on a brochure distinguishes them.

This is why delivery record belongs in due diligence rather than in hindsight. A developer's history of hitting or missing its own milestone schedule is checkable, and it is a better predictor of your handover date than the date printed on the floor plan.

## The part that actually hurts an overseas buyer

A delay you know about in month four is a planning problem. A delay you learn about in month twenty-six is a financial one — it lands on mortgage arrangements, rental income assumptions, and any onward plan that assumed a handover quarter.

An overseas buyer with nobody monitoring construction on their behalf typically finds out a project has slipped when the developer chooses to say so. Progress is not secret: the DLD publishes a project tracker, and milestone certification is a matter of record. It simply requires someone to be watching it, month after month, years after the commission on the sale was paid.

That is the whole argument for [construction-phase monitoring](/lifecycle) being part of the service rather than a favour. And it is the stage where a commission-only agent has the least reason to keep working, because the work is already paid for.
