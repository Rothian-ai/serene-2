Announced supply and delivered supply are different numbers, and the gap between them is where off-plan buyers make or lose their assumptions. Headlines this year have put Dubai's 2027 pipeline at anything up to 90,000 units. The escrow record tells a quieter story.

## The filing test

A development is real when its escrow account is funded and its milestone schedule is filed. Apply that filter: funded escrow, filed milestones, active site. The 2027 pipeline then contracts to roughly two-thirds of the announced figure. The remainder is marketing: land with a render attached.

## Where the real units sit

Three districts carry most of the credible 2027 handovers: Creek Harbour, where infrastructure landed ahead of population; the Meydan-Hartland corridor, where mid-rise supply is genuinely abundant; and the established Downtown fringe, where single towers infill a mature market.

The composition matters more than the total. Creek Harbour's deliveries arrive into a district still being priced, historically the profile where early buyers capture repricing. Hartland's arrive into direct competition, which disciplines rents. Downtown's arrive into depth, which absorbs them.

## What we'd watch

Not the launch announcements. The milestone filings. A developer that files late in quarter one delivers late in quarter four; the correlation in the public record is strong enough to treat as a rule.
